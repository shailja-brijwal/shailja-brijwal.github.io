Case Study

Predicting Early Student Risk

How early digital engagement signals can help identify students who may need support — while there is still time to intervene.

The Challenge

Using early student engagement data, our challenge was to identify student-course pairs at higher risk of unsuccessful course outcomes while the university still had time to provide meaningful support.

The Scale

The analysis brought together more than 12 million daily engagement records, representing over 117,000 student-course pairs across multiple faculties.

My Role

As Project Lead and Modelling Co-Lead, I helped shape the analytical approach, develop and evaluate predictive models, interpret results, and translate the analysis into recommendations for stakeholders.

The Approach

We engineered behavioural features from early engagement patterns and compared an interpretable logistic regression baseline with XGBoost. Model performance was assessed alongside explainability and practical usefulness.

Key Result

ROC-AUC improved from 0.79 to 0.86

The final XGBoost model achieved approximately 0.67 recall and 0.33 precision at the selected operating threshold, while reducing false positives by around 3,000 compared with the baseline approach at similar recall.

From prediction to decision

The goal was not simply to build the most accurate model. The solution needed to be interpretable, equitable and operationally useful. The analysis therefore focused on identifying meaningful engagement patterns and translating model outputs into insights that could support targeted student interventions.
